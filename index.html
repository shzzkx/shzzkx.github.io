Speaker Box Project

MechanicalElectronicsSoftware

AutoCAD, Seeeduino Nano, C++

In a team of 4, I designed a bass-reflex speaker cabinet to get the loudest sound from a given driver and one sheet of cardboard. I found the angle for a slanted top panel that reflects more sound into the port, while my teammates drafted the cutouts in AutoCAD. We then tested waveforms and frequencies with measurement distance and background noise held constant, and found a 100 Hz sine wave gave the loudest output, 120.3 dB compared with the driver's 97.0 dB baseline. Our cutting errors stacked up and the panels didn't fit cleanly, which taught me to design in manufacturing tolerances for the future.

The Aurum Theorem: Mousetrap Car

Mechanical

Prototyping, testing, mechanical design

I designed and built a car powered only by a single mousetrap for a grade 11 physics project. I tested 10+ prototypes spanning 4 design concepts (using cardboard, popsicle sticks, and 2 hybrid designs) and chose a design made only of popsicle sticks based on performance data. I researched and tested drivetrain lengths, materials and joint attachments to transfer energy more efficiently, and more than doubled the car's travel distance, from 7.16 m to 16.92 m. The final car drives straight within a 0.6 m-wide lane. This was my most challenging high school project, but it was also the most rewarding, both in terms of the distance I achieved and what I learned: how to apply physics and material properties to mechanical design, and how to improve with each prototype.

Arduino Pong Game

ElectronicsSoftware

Arduino UNO R4, C++

A Pong game I implemented on an Arduino board using an OLED display and push buttons in a team of 2. I was responsible for programming the game logic, including the ball movement, collision detection, and scoring system. When the ball passed through paddles, we logged coordinates over the serial monitor and found that at high speeds, the ball skipped past the paddle between frames. We fixed this issue by capping the ball's speed.
